  What a Chance card IS

  A Chance card is an interrupt — a moment that breaks the predictable flow of play and injects a named, authored
  personality into the game. It isn't a random stat modifier; it's a tiny story with a rule attached. The name carries
  the feeling; the instruction carries the mechanic.

  The Anatomy of a Great Card

  NAME         — evocative, punny, or cultural reference (not descriptive)
  IMMEDIATE    — fires once on draw, OR
  DURATION     — lasts until a condition ("for the rest of the game", "until X happens")
  TARGET       — YOU / ONE OPPONENT (your choice) / ALL PLAYERS
  DIRECTION    — boon, burden, or chaos (many are chaos — neither pure good nor bad)
  SOCIAL LAYER — optional: requires a physical or behavioral act on top of the mechanic
  HIDDEN       — optional: a second instruction revealed only after committing

  The best cards combine a mechanical layer with a behavioral layer. The Bartender gives you nothing mechanical — it's
  pure role-play. The Masseuse gives you one board modification and makes you serve others. Dwarven gives a resource
  bonus and forces you to sit on the floor. The social act is the real content.

  ---
  Principles That Survive Translation to Video Games

  1. Names evoke feel, not function. "Blue Shell" is not called "City Downgrade." "Waterfall" is not called "Sequential
  Mandatory Drink." The name comes first — it's a brand.
  2. Duration is condition-based, not timed. Cards last until something happens ("until you roll that number again,"
  "until someone builds"), not "for 30 seconds." This creates tension and narrative.
  3. Chaos cards are the most memorable. Shuffle Up and Deal, Fuck the Numbers, Carousel — the cards that scramble the
  entire game state are talked about forever. Pure chaos is a valid and beloved archetype.
  4. Power is wildly asymmetric. Slam Fucking Dunk is worth 8 victory points. Crusty Bitch makes you eat someone's pizza
   crust. Both are legitimate cards. The asymmetry is the point — drawing a card is exciting because you don't know
  which one you'll get.
  5. Cards that target the leader are universally loved. Blue Shell is a Mario Kart reference for a reason. High Roller,
   Incite Revolt, Snaked — the game self-balances through card chaos.
  6. Holdable cards create meta-game tension. Knowing someone has a Cancel card changes how everyone plays. The card
  does work just by existing in someone's hand.
  7. Hidden instructions add a reveal moment. The card already fired — but then there's a second shoe to drop. This is
  pure drama.
  8. Player-specific cards (Reparations) are negative cards earned through losing — they function as a catch-up penalty
  on the player who already fell behind. They're not random; they're punitive and thematic.

  ---
  The Irreducible Core

  A Chance card is: a named moment that injects personality, breaks expectation, and reassigns power — either instantly
  or through an ongoing rule that colors every subsequent action. The mechanical effect is secondary to the feeling of
  drawing it. The best ones make the whole table react.

  For an LLM generating a video game with a Chance mechanic, the generative formula is:

  ▎ [Evocative Name] + [One-sentence rule] + [Optional: duration condition] + [Optional: behavioral/social layer] +
  ▎ [Optional: hidden second effect]
